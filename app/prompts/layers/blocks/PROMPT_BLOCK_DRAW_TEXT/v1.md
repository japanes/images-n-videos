Write the words into the picture itself, as real lettering that belongs to the scene.

Set them in the language they are given, character for character: every letter, accent and punctuation mark exactly as written, nothing translated, shortened, rephrased or added. They are the only text anywhere in the frame.

Form every glyph fully and cleanly — even letter spacing, even line spacing, one alignment per block of words, each block written once and in one place. Keep the line breaks where they are, and in a list keep one item per line with the marker it carries.

Put the words where they read at a glance: on a calm, even part of the picture, clear of faces and of the product, with air between them and every edge of the frame, and with enough contrast against what is behind them to be read without effort.

{{#if words}}
The words to write. The name in square brackets above each block says how that block is set and is not text to draw: headline is the largest lettering in the frame and is read first, body is clearly smaller and set for reading, accent is small but stands apart by weight or colour.
