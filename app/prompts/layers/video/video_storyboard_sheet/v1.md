Storyboard for a {{seconds}}-second {{aspect}} video: {{panels}} panels on one sheet, {{cols}} across and {{rows}} down, read left to right and top to bottom, in the order of the film.
The whole film runs {{seconds}} seconds, and the panels divide those seconds between them: they are moments of one film, not separate clips, and none of them has a length of its own to work out.
Lay it out as a working storyboard sheet, the kind a director is handed. Across the top, a header strip: the word STORYBOARD, how long the film runs ({{seconds}} seconds) and what shape it is ({{aspect}}). Under it the panels, each one in the film's own shape ({{aspect}}), separated by thin plain gutters on a plain sheet.
Each panel is a frame of the finished film, and it looks the way that film looks when it is shot: a photographic still with real light, real lenses and real depth of field, the people and the things in it exactly as the photographs of them show. It is not a comic, not a cartoon, not an ink or pencil drawing, and nothing inside the frames is outlined by hand — the only drawn thing on the sheet is the lettering. The one exception is the look asked for below: if the film itself is drawn or animated, the panels are frames of that film.
Every panel is one panel of the list below, in that order, and every panel is lettered, in {{language}}:
- the panel number, 01 upwards, in a corner of the frame;
- the timecode of that panel on a small chip beside the number, copied exactly from the list of timecodes below;
- under the frame, a title of two or three words in capitals, and one or two short lines saying what happens.
Letter it small, straight and even, in a plain sans-serif hand, with every word spelled correctly and nothing running over the edge of its panel. Nothing else is written anywhere: no speech bubbles, no arrows or notes over the frames, no logos, no page border.
